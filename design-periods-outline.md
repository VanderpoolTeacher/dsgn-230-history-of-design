# Survey of Design Periods: Industrial Revolution to Present
## Course Outline — DRAFT

**Audience:** College-level design students
**Format:** Lesson + Interactive (SCORM-packaged)
**Scope:** High-level survey — key movements, defining characteristics, context, legacy

---

## Module Structure

### 1. Introduction — Why Design History Matters
- Design as a response to technology, culture, and economics
- Framing question: *How does each era's design reflect what society values?*

### 2. The Industrial Revolution & Early Design Reform (c. 1760–1880)
- Mass production and the "crisis of taste"
- The Great Exhibition of 1851
- Key figures: William Morris, John Ruskin
- Transition point: handcraft vs. machine

### 3. Arts & Crafts Movement (c. 1880–1910)
- Reaction against industrialization
- Emphasis on craftsmanship, natural forms, honesty of materials
- Influence on architecture, furniture, typography
- Key works and practitioners

### 4. Art Nouveau (c. 1890–1910)
- Organic line, asymmetry, total design (Gesamtkunstwerk)
- Regional variations: Jugendstil, Stile Liberty, Modernisme
- Key figures: Mucha, Guimard, Gaudí, Tiffany

### 5. Early Modernism & the Avant-Garde (c. 1910–1930)
- Futurism, Constructivism, De Stijl
- Form follows function — the emergence of functionalism
- Key figures: Marinetti, Rodchenko, Mondrian, Rietveld

### 6. Bauhaus (1919–1933)
- Unifying art, craft, and technology
- Three directors, three directions: Gropius, Meyer, Mies
- Pedagogy as design philosophy
- Lasting influence on graphic design, architecture, industrial design

### 7. Art Deco (c. 1920–1940)
- Glamour, geometry, and modernity
- Contrast with Bauhaus austerity
- Materials: chrome, lacquer, exotic woods
- Influence on architecture, fashion, cinema

### 8. Mid-Century Modern (c. 1945–1970)
- Post-war optimism and democratic design
- Scandinavian design and organic modernism
- Key figures: Eames, Saarinen, Jacobsen, Rams
- The golden age of industrial design

### 9. Postmodernism (c. 1970–1990)
- Rejection of modernist orthodoxy
- Irony, decoration, historical reference
- Memphis Group, Venturi, Graves
- "Less is a bore"

### 10. Digital Revolution & Contemporary Design (c. 1990–Present)
- The screen as design surface
- Web design, UX/UI, interaction design
- Flat design, material design, design systems
- Sustainability, inclusive design, AI-assisted design
- Key question: *Where is design headed next?*

### 11. Interactive Component — Timeline Explorer & Knowledge Check
- **Timeline:** Draggable/scrollable timeline with representative images, key dates, and short descriptions per period
- **Matching Activity:** Match design characteristics to their movement
- **Compare & Contrast:** Side-by-side viewer — select two periods and see differences in philosophy, materials, and form
- **Quiz:** 10–15 questions (multiple choice + image identification) for SCORM scoring/completion

## SCORM Considerations
- Track: quiz score (pass/fail threshold) + completion status
- Bookmarking: allow learners to resume where they left off
- Accessibility: alt text for all images, keyboard navigation, screen reader support
- Responsive: must work on tablets and desktops
